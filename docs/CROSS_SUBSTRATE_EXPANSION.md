# Cross-Substrate Expansion

## Role

Cross-substrate expansion remains a comparative transport program, not a solved
transport-preservation program.

- `Stage 2-M2` made transport blockage runtime-loadable.
- `Stage 2-M3` added transition-contract operationalization, preservation-axis readiness tracking, and fork or fracture risk accounting.
- `Stage 2-M4` audited that transition-contract layer and kept Stage 3 unopened while target-side counterparts remained absent.
- `Stage3-RF` binds transport-side schemas, lineage maps, and error budgets tightly enough that Stage 3 may now open without authorizing any transport-preservation verdict.

Identity-preservation outputs remain forbidden.

## Runtime classes

The transport protocol still defines:

- `transport_not_open`
- `structurally_preserving_candidate`
- `partially_preserving_candidate`
- `fractured_transport`
- `forked_transport`
- `unsupported_remap`
- `comparator_underdetermined_transport`

The readiness layer now also distinguishes:

- `stage3_prerequisites_partially_satisfied`
- `stage3_ready_but_uninstantiated`
- `ready_for_stage3_opening`

## Current runtime meaning

The cross-substrate runtime can now say:

- no source-target transport verdict exists in the adjudicative sense;
- transition contracts are formally defined and machine-readable;
- preservation axes have explicit budget and lineage bindings;
- transport-side schemas and lineage maps are declared;
- Stage 3 may now open to instantiate the required target-side counterpart family;
- transport remains uninstantiated rather than preserved.

## What remains blocked

This layer still does not authorize:

- cross-substrate equivalence;
- identity preservation;
- fracture verdicts as metaphysical conclusions;
- fork verdicts as identity conclusions;
- external adjudication;
- Paper 10 transport claims.
