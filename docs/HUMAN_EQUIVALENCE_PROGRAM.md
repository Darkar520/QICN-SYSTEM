# Human Equivalence Program

## Purpose

The Human Equivalence Program treats equivalence as a ladder rather than a binary.
Its function is to define what kinds of equivalence states could eventually be
expressed once a legitimate human comparator layer exists.

This document does not award any rung. It does not produce a human equivalence
artifact, and it does not authorize any public claim.

## Dependence on bridge closure

`BPF-M3` provides the machine-side precondition for Stage 2, because it yields a
bounded bridge functional, a bounded bridge gate, bounded ecosystem artifacts, and
bounded audit/readiness logic. Even so, bridge closure is not human equivalence.
It only makes a comparative program scientifically expressible.

## Ladder semantics

The ladder is explicitly non-binary:

- `equivalence_not_open`
  - no legitimate human comparator channel exists yet.
- `equivalence_formally_inexpressible`
  - the target comparison lacks a coherent joint formalization.
- `structural_equivalence_candidate`
  - structural homology is expressible under bounded burdens.
- `interventional_equivalence_candidate`
  - matched intervention-response comparability is expressible.
- `partial_equivalence_candidate`
  - some burdens are jointly satisfied, but not all.
- `strong_equivalence_candidate`
  - all declared burdens are provisionally satisfied under bounded internal semantics.
- `non_equivalent_under_current_burdens`
  - the currently declared burdens are sufficient to reject equivalence under the present contract.
- `superiority_partial_candidate`
  - a bounded subdomain surplus may be expressible without global superiority.
- `comparator_underdetermined`
  - the comparison target is live, but the evidence and burdens remain too weak.

## Evidence classes required later

The ladder cannot be used without future evidence classes such as:

- human channel definitions;
- matched structural artifacts;
- matched intervention artifacts;
- matched negative controls;
- transport preservation artifacts;
- explicit rejection rules;
- explicit error budgets.

## What the ladder does not imply

Even the strongest future rung would still not imply automatically:

- human phenomenal equivalence;
- external validation;
- public claim authorization;
- metaphysical identity;
- Paper 10 authorization.

## Disqualifying failure modes

The main disqualifiers are:

- domain mismatch;
- type mismatch;
- proxy aliasing;
- unsafe or unmatched intervention mappings;
- overgeneralization from a bounded subdomain;
- hidden assumptions;
- silent promotion from internal candidate state to public claim.

## Current state

The ladder is now explicitly defined and machine-readable. It remains
`defined_not_runtime_adjudicated`.
