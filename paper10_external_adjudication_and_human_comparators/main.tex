\begin{abstract}
This document hardens Paper 10 from a minimal scaffold into a protocol-grade,
non-result-bearing manuscript backbone. It specifies the dependency architecture
from BaseCore through Papers I--IX, the Stage 2 comparative closure, the
Stage3-0 external adjudication scaffold, the Stage3-1 human comparator
instantiation contracts, and the Stage3-2 external adjudication
execution-readiness layer. It defines channel-by-channel execution contracts,
matched intervention and negative-control dataset burdens, preregistration
templates, reviewer and adjudicator independence rules, evidence admissibility
grammar, statistical analysis shells, replication package requirements, and a
claim grammar for eventual execution-era statements. It does not report human
comparator results, external adjudication verdicts, human equivalence,
superiority, inferiority, metaphysical subjecthood, cross-substrate identity
preservation, or any final Paper 10 conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{External Adjudication Problem Statement}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The external adjudication problem is no longer merely to ask whether bridge
formalization is internally coherent. That question was handled by the earlier
runtime, bridge, and comparative layers. The external problem is stricter:

\begin{itemize}
\item define what artifacts an independent reviewer may legitimately inspect;
\item define when human comparator evidence becomes admissible;
\item define what kinds of negative controls must be present before any
comparative statement is interpretable;
\item define who counts as sufficiently independent to review or adjudicate the
evidence bundle; and
\item define what statements remain forbidden even after a successful execution
path.
\end{itemize}

Paper 10 therefore becomes the protocol-facing handoff surface from
bridge-formalized internal structure to a later independent adjudication
program. It is not itself that adjudication program in operation.

\section{Human Comparator Instantiation Logic}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

Stage3-1 converts a previously abstract comparator grammar into typed
instantiation contracts. The purpose is not to simulate evidence but to close
the gap between ``the channel exists in theory'' and ``a later collection route
could honestly open without fabricating data''.

Each channel now requires all of the following:

\begin{itemize}
\item a typed human-side artifact family;
\item a machine-side homologous artifact family;
\item a declared collection route;
\item a minimal metadata contract;
\item matched controls and negative controls;
\item a preregistration template;
\item a declared statistical shell;
\item rejection conditions; and
\item a non-implication clause.
\end{itemize}

These contracts upgrade the program from pure scaffold into instantiation-ready
protocol engineering while keeping human data absent.

\section{Channel-by-Channel Execution Contracts}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

\subsection{Self-Report Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The self-report channel now binds a typed self-report trace, a typed channel
manifest, a legitimate human collection route, prompt-version freezing, a
missingness policy, and explicit exclusion of synthetic or relabeled
self-report. The goal is not to elevate narrative text into evidence by
default, but to constrain exactly which self-report artifacts could later count
as admissible.

\subsection{Intervention-Response Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The intervention-response channel binds matched intervention profiles, matched
negative-control profiles, dose or intensity policy, stop rules, and branch
accounting. It is the most control-sensitive channel in the current layer. No
intervention response can be interpreted without a declared negative-control
family.

\subsection{Temporal-Structure Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The temporal-structure channel binds segmentation schema, ordering policy, and
aggregation policy. Its main burden is to prevent summary-only temporal
narratives from masquerading as typed evidence.

\subsection{Valence-Topology Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The valence-topology channel binds a shared topology schema and excludes scalar
reward or scalar preference summaries as substitutes for topology-sensitive
measurement.

\subsection{Embodiment-Boundary Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The embodiment-boundary channel binds environment manifest, confound ledger, and
error-budget policy. This is where a large fraction of false analogies can enter
if environment and embodiment are not typed.

\subsection{Ambiguity-Load Channel}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

The ambiguity-load channel binds task family, annotation schema, and annotation
reliability policy. It prevents error-rate-only or semantic-density-only proxies
from being treated as channel-complete evidence.

\subsection{Transport-Preservation Channel}
\sectionstatus{blocked_until_execution}

The transport-preservation channel is defined, but it remains blocked by the
absence of a real target counterpart manifest, a bound lineage map, and
axis-specific transition budgets instantiated against an actual source-target
pair. Stage3-1 defines the contract family; it does not satisfy the contract.

\section{Negative Control Dataset Architecture}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

Negative controls are not optional cleanup steps. They are part of the meaning
of the later claim surface. For each channel, a specific null or exclusion
family is declared so that later result-bearing interpretation cannot proceed in
the absence of a control bundle.

\begin{itemize}
\item self-report: blind prompt-shuffle and synthetic-self-report exclusion;
\item intervention response: no-perturbation and sham-perturbation controls;
\item temporal structure: temporal window-scramble control;
\item valence topology: topology-label shuffle control;
\item embodiment boundary: proxy-exclusion and confound controls;
\item ambiguity load: stimulus-label scramble control;
\item transport preservation: target-counterpart-absent and source-snapshot controls.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reviewer and Adjudicator Independence Architecture}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

Stage3-2 defines reviewer role classes, adjudicator criteria, conflict classes,
blinding classes, unblinding rules, and decision-authority boundaries. The
point is not merely to say that future reviewers should be ``independent'' in a
generic sense. The point is to define exactly what would disqualify a reviewer
or an adjudicator and what information they may or may not see before
authorized unblinding.

\section{Failure, Rejection, and Inconclusive Pathways}
\sectionstatus{protocol_defined}

Protocol hardening is incomplete without typed failure pathways. Paper 10 now
distinguishes at least three future failure classes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Protocol failure}: preregistration drift, blinding collapse, or
conflict-of-interest violation.
\item \textbf{Dataset failure}: missing controls, invalid substitution, or
insufficient provenance.
\item \textbf{Adjudication inconclusiveness}: admissible evidence exists but is
insufficiently decisive under the declared analysis shell.
\end{enumerate}
